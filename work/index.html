How I design AI features

AI is what I design, and it’s also how I design. It’s with me from the first question to the last look back.

It starts with research: users, the market, the competition. AI helps me develop my personas and build the concept. When it’s time to prototype, I make something that really works.

When the project is over, AI and I sit down for a retro. We look at how it went and what I can take into the next one.

Being its user this often is useful. I know what I want from AI, and it goes straight into the features I design.

How I prototype with AI

This portfolio is my latest prototype. I designed it in Figma and built it with Claude Code, the way I prototype: working pages in place of static frames.

  • Research first. AI read 15 sources on what hiring teams look for in 2026, and checked my site against them.
  • Options, then my call. AI made eight visual directions as mood boards. I chose one, mixed in parts of others, and rejected what felt messy.
  • A design system in Figma. Colors, type and components as variables and styles, with the same names as in the site’s code.
  • Figma as the source of truth. Claude Code builds the pages from my frames, and a small tool compares the site’s text with Figma so the two don’t drift apart.
  • Accessibility, measured. Every text color is checked against WCAG AA, not by eye.
  • The last word is mine. When the AI gets a fact wrong or says too much, I fix it before it goes live.
