About

Fourteen years making complicated software behave

I started in an Air Force software unit that had no designers, and spent three years convincing it that it needed one. Since then the work has mostly been the same shape: dense operational systems, real consequences, users who are busy and under pressure.

What I am good at

Building design functions that do not exist yet

I pitched a UX practice to the head of my Air Force unit and was turned down, so I did it after hours as a volunteer until the demand made the argument for me. I became the first UX designer in the Israeli Air Force unofficially, then officially three years later. I put UX into the unit's software methodology, ran the first UX conference held in the IDF, and founded the design wing with funding in the millions to staff it. At Asperii I hired three designers, trained them and led them.

Designing AI that ships

Four years at Salesforce designing production AI inside Service Cloud, not prototypes and not demos. Six features across six engineering teams spread over India, Israel and San Francisco, and for part of that time I was the only designer on all of them.

The interesting part is never the model. It is what the interface does when the model is wrong, and how much of its reasoning a person needs to see before they will put their name on the output.
