About

Fourteen years making complicated software behave

I started in an Air Force software unit that had no designers and spent three years convincing it that it needed one. The work since has mostly had the same shape. Systems people depend on at speed, where being wrong shows up somewhere real.

What I am good at

Building design functions that do not exist yet

I pitched a UX practice to the head of my Air Force unit and was turned down, so I did it after hours as a volunteer until the demand made the argument for me. I became the first UX designer in the Israeli Air Force unofficially, then officially three years later. I put UX into the unit's software methodology and ran the first UX conference held in the IDF. Then I founded the design wing and got funding in the millions to staff it. At Asperii I built a design team of three from nothing.

Designing AI that ships

Four years at Salesforce on AI that shipped. Six features, six engineering teams, India and Israel and San Francisco. For part of it I was the only designer across all six.

The question underneath all of them was how much the machine should do on its own. I ended up working on it at four points on the same scale: training a model and choosing which one runs, drafting something for a person to approve, acting without asking, and deciding what the thing is allowed to touch in the first place.

Changing how the work ships

My last stretch of work went out as running prototypes built on our design system, rather than as Figma files with a document explaining them. Eighty-four commits over six weeks, and the last one was a bug fix.
