What a good recommendation actually says

Most people freeze when asked, and write something generic about being lovely to work with. Send them two or three lines to start from and they'll write something useful in five minutes. Three things worth them saying:

  • The scope. That you were the only designer across six engineering teams in three countries. Nobody reading your CV will believe that until someone else says it.
  • One judgment call they watched you make. An argument you won, a direction you pushed back on, a thing you insisted on. Specific beats warm.
  • How you worked, if they saw it. That you sent running prototypes instead of Figma files, and what that changed for them.

Lines they can adapt:

"Stav was the only designer across our whole area, six teams in three time zones, and somehow it never showed."

"She stopped handing us Figma files and started handing us prototypes we could click through. It changed how we ran reviews."

"When we wanted to ship [x], she was the one who argued we shouldn't until we'd solved [y]. She was right."

Who to ask: PMs you worked with closely, an engineering lead or two, and your manager. Four or five people is plenty. More than that and they get thin.
Ask the PMs for the three golden numbers same email You have no numbers anywhere in the portfolio right now. Three across the whole thing is plenty. Twenty is not twice as good.
Which three, and why those
1. Acceptance rate How often a rep sent the AI's draft without editing it. This is the one that says the design worked. If people take the suggestion as-is, they trusted it. 2. Edit rate How often they changed it before sending. This is a design metric, not a model metric, and almost nobody thinks to ask for it. If most drafts get edited, the model was fine and the handoff wasn't. That distinction is exactly what an interviewer wants to hear you make. 3. Adoption How many orgs or agents turned it on. Proves it shipped and that someone wanted it.

Nice to have, not worth chasing: time saved per case, deflection rate, CSAT.

Rough is completely fine. "Around 60%" is worth having. "Directionally it went up a lot" is worth having. A launch readout, a QBR slide or a screenshot of a dashboard all count.

If the answer is that nobody measured it, that is also a real answer and I want to know. On the page we say we never instrumented it and what I'd push for now, which survives a follow-up question. A vague claim like "response times improved" does not.
